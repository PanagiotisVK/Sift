Submission ID 091bf87b-af03-442a-b5ac-61d0bcc6acf0, rejected 5 August 2026 under 4.3(a).

The rejection turns on resemblance to other apps but identifies none. I asked in the Resolution Center on 5 August which app Sift was found to resemble, and which screens or metadata prompted it. There has been no reply. I cannot change what is too close while I do not know what it is.

Three of the four listed factors are verifiable on Apple's side. Sift is the only app I have ever submitted, under one account and one bundle ID. There is no second account. The code is my own, written from scratch, with no purchased template or white-label SDK; Capacitor is the native wrapper and nothing more.

That leaves concept. A swipe interface over previews is a familiar surface. What runs underneath it is not.

1. Hook detection. Sift decodes every 30-second preview through the Web Audio API, computes an energy envelope in 0.25s frames, smooths it so sustained energy beats a lone transient, excludes the closing seconds so playback cannot land in a fade, and starts 1.5s before the peak. The listener hears the chorus, not the intro. The same analysis draws each card's waveform.

2. Adaptive discovery depth. Sift measures how much of what it shows a listener they already recognise, and uses that to decide how far past the popular results to reach. Two listeners entering identical taste are shown materially different music.

3. An open-data recommendation graph, built from ListenBrainz co-listening data and MusicBrainz tags, with alias resolution for artists filed under non-Latin names. Not a store's built-in "similar artists" field.

The iTunes Search API supplies preview audio and artwork, used as documented, with links out to the store. It is the playback layer, not the product.

I ask the Board either to identify the app or apps Sift was found to resemble, so I can address it, or to overturn the finding. I will make any change warranted; I need only to know what it is.
